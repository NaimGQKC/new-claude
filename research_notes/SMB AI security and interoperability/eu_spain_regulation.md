# EU and Spanish regulation of AI assistants used by employees in SMBs (as of 20 November 2026)

Method note: research was done via web search/fetch. Most legal-text sources were secondary (law-firm/blog summaries) because EUR-Lex returned empty content and two law-firm pages returned 403. Where a statement rests only on secondary sources, it is flagged. Statements marked "background" in Inferences come from general knowledge of the GDPR/AI Act text and were not re-verified in this session; the report writer should treat them as lower confidence.

## 1. EU AI Act timeline, Digital Omnibus, and what applies to deployers and GPAI providers

### Takeaway
The Digital Omnibus on AI is no longer a proposal: it was adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 and is in force since 27 July 2026 (per multiple secondary sources). It delays standalone Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and Annex I product-embedded ones to 2 August 2028, softens Article 4 AI literacy from an outcome duty to a "take measures" duty, and leaves GPAI obligations and most Article 50 transparency timing in place. A typical SMB using an AI chat assistant is a low-risk "deployer" whose real duties today are AI literacy measures, GDPR, and (if it publishes AI-generated content or deploys chatbots toward people) Article 50 transparency.

### Cited Findings
- Parliament and Council reached provisional agreement on 7 May 2026; Parliament endorsed 16 June 2026; Council final approval 29 June 2026; adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, published in the OJ 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026. — [Verifywise summary (provisional stage)](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed); [Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay-high-risk-december-2027/); [White & Case "EU AI Omnibus enters into force"](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act) (title only; page body returned 403). Official text not retrieved (EUR-Lex ELI given by Ecorpit: eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj).
- New dates: standalone Annex III high-risk systems 2 August 2026 -> 2 December 2027; AI embedded in Annex I regulated products (e.g. medical devices) 2 August 2027 -> 2 August 2028; national AI regulatory sandboxes -> 2 August 2027. — [Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay-high-risk-december-2027/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/) (search snippet)
- Article 4 (AI literacy) stays but is softened: providers and deployers must "take measures to support" a sufficient level of AI literacy of staff rather than "ensure" it (obligation of conduct, not result); Commission and Member States must support and facilitate (e.g. practical examples). The earlier Commission proposal to drop the binding duty was not what was finally adopted. Applies since 2 February 2025. — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act) (via search summary); [Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay-high-risk-december-2027/); [Fruggr](https://www.fruggr.io/en/ai-literacy-article-4-after-omnibus-2026/); [Law and Technology](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/ai-literacy-digital-omnibus-article-4-ai-act/)
- Article 50: obligations applied from 2 August 2026; providers of generative AI systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 get until 2 December 2026 for machine-readable marking/watermarking (Art. 50(2)); the Commission had proposed 2 February 2027. Systems placed on market from 2 August 2026 get no grace period. — [Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay-high-risk-december-2027/). (Verifywise's provisional-stage summary describes Art. 50(2) as moving from 2 Aug to 2 Dec 2026 — slightly different framing; the final-text grace period is limited to legacy systems.)
- Omnibus adds an Article 5 prohibition on AI systems generating non-consensual intimate imagery/CSAM ("nudifier" apps), with a safe harbour for effective safeguards (reported from provisional agreement). — [Verifywise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed)
- SME relief: fines for SMEs capped at the lower of fixed amount/turnover percentage (Art. 99(6)); extended to small mid-caps for the EUR 15m and 7.5m tiers (new Art. 99(6a)), not for the Art. 5 tier; SME support measures extended to small mid-caps. — [Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay-high-risk-december-2027/)
- GPAI: Chapter V obligations (Arts. 51-55) have applied since 2 August 2025; fines on GPAI providers under Art. 101 apply from 2 August 2026; AI Office competence clarified for AI systems built on GPAI models by the same provider and for AI in very large online platforms/search engines. — [Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay-high-risk-december-2027/); [Verifywise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed)
- Exemptions for free/open-source GPAI models do not apply to GPAI models with systemic risk; most self-hosting organisations are deployers rather than GPAI providers (vendor blog claim). — [Qovery](https://www.qovery.com/blog/eu-ai-act-gpai-enforcement-self-hosting-open-weight-models); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/eu-ai-act-gpai-deepseek-review)
- Commentary consensus: the Omnibus changes timing, not substance; "buys time but removes no obligations". — [search summary of Usercentrics/CSA/DLA Piper results](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/)

### Inferences
- Background (not re-verified): deployer duties for low-risk tools are Art. 4 literacy plus Art. 50(3)-(4) (inform people exposed to emotion recognition/biometric categorisation; label deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest unless human-reviewed/editorially controlled). Art. 50(1) (tell people they are interacting with AI) falls on providers/deployers of chatbots facing customers. Prohibited practices (Art. 5, incl. emotion recognition in the workplace) have applied since 2 Feb 2025 and are relevant to HR-style uses.
- Background: Annex III high-risk covers employment/worker management (recruitment, CV screening, performance evaluation, task allocation) and access to essential services; an SMB using a general chat assistant for drafting is not high-risk, but repurposing it for candidate scoring would be. The AEPD warning on CV-screening AI (section 3) shows regulators already police this via GDPR even though AI Act high-risk duties are delayed to Dec 2027.
- A vendor can make Art. 4 easy by shipping role-based training modules, usage disclosures, and training-completion records; the "obligation of conduct" wording makes documentary evidence of measures taken the practical compliance artifact.

### Gaps
- Official text of Regulation 2026/1744 (exact Art. 4 wording, article numbering) was not retrieved; all details are from secondary sources which agree with each other. Verify on EUR-Lex before quoting.
- Exact Art. 50(1)/(3)/(4) application date for non-legacy systems was reported as 2 Aug 2026 by only one source (Ecorpit).

## 2. GDPR obligations when employees use AI chat tools (roles, DPA, lawful basis, DPIA, transfers)

### Takeaway
The employer SMB is controller; the AI vendor is normally processor under Art. 28 for business/API/enterprise tiers (consumer tiers make the vendor an independent controller, which is the shadow-AI problem). Regulator guidance (EDPB Opinion 28/2024, AEPD guidance) emphasises legitimate-interest assessments, DPIA where high risk, minimisation, human oversight and need-to-know access; there is no Spanish "AI-chat-at-work" rule beyond general GDPR/LOPDGDD, but AEPD publishes its own staff policy and sector guidance that SMBs can copy.

### Cited Findings
- EDPB Opinion 28/2024: whether an AI model trained on personal data is anonymous is case-by-case with a high threshold; legitimate interest requires the three-step test (interest, necessity, balancing); a conversational assistant is cited as a plausible legitimate-interest use; the initial unlawfulness of model training must be considered in the balancing and, when a different controller deploys the model, that deployer must also assess it. — [EDPB Opinion 28/2024 PDF](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf); [CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/edpb-opinion-28-2024-key-takeaways-on-processing-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/operational-impacts-of-the-edpb-s-opinion-on-data-protection-in-ai-models) (full deployer test not extracted).
- AEPD published (Nov 2025 version, with a Jan 2026 4-page summary) its own internal generative-AI policy for its staff; reported rule: staff may use AI for general tasks but may not enter personal or confidential data into any AI system (press summary; verify against original — PDF could not be text-extracted). — [AEPD press release](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/agencia-publica-politica-interna-uso-inteligencia-artificial); [AEPD policy PDF](https://www.aepd.es/documento/politica-iag-aepd.pdf); [AEPD summary PDF (EN)](https://www.aepd.es/en/guides/summary-recommendations-iag-aepd.pdf); [Vandal press summary](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/r38726/espana-se-adelanta-a-toda-europa-y-publica-las-primeras-normas-oficiales-sobre-el-uso-de-la-ia-sin-poner-en-riesgo-tus-datos)
- AEPD guide on agentic AI (Feb 2026): controller must assess risk; set autonomy level per processing; need-to-know access, data classification/labelling, memory compartmentalised per processing and user, strict retention; "rule of two" (no more than two of: uncontrolled input, unrestricted access to sensitive data, automatic actions with real effects); warns of "shadow-leak" and prompt injection; recommends DPO involvement and integrity-protected logs. — [Cuatrecasas analysis (4 Mar 2026)](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/latam/propiedad-intelectual/art/guia-aepd-inteligencia-artificial-agentica); [CMS Spain](https://cms.law/en/esp/publication/inteligencia-artificial-agentica.-la-agencia-espanola-de-proteccion-de-datos-publica-unas-orientaciones-sobre-su-uso)
- AEPD preventive warning (AI-00009-2026, 23 Sep 2026) to a company considering AI to screen CVs/score candidates: requires data protection by design, risk assessment and prior DPIA if high risk, transparency to candidates/workers ("información clara, accesible y comprensible"), effective human intervention (not "asumir automáticamente el resultado"), and respect for the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions. Not an infringement finding. — [AEPD press note](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/agencia-recuerda-garantias-para-utilizar-ia-en-analisis-curriculums); [AEPD warning PDF](https://www.aepd.es/documento/ai-00009-2026-advertencia.pdf)
- AEPD fined Aena EUR 10,043,002 for deploying facial recognition at airports without a DPIA (illustrates DPIA enforcement; biometric, not a chat assistant). — [search summary of AEPD/Ecija sources](https://www.ecija.com/actualidad-insights/mas-sanciones-y-de-mayor-importe-la-aepd-sube-el-nivel-de-multas-en-2025/) (fine amount from secondary summary; verify).
- AEPD also published material on quality, accuracy and minimisation of personal data in AI systems. — [AEPD press note](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/aepd-analiza-calidad-exactitud-y-minimizacion-de-datos-personales-en-tratamientos-con-ia)
- Regional Spanish public-administration practical guides to generative AI at work exist (Junta de Castilla y León) — useful as templates. — [JCYL guide v6](https://eclapgestiondelconocimiento.jcyl.es/sites/default/files/2026-06/ECLAP-Gui%CC%81a-IAGenerativa-V6.pdf)

### Inferences
- Background (GDPR text, not re-fetched): Art. 4(7)/(8) controller/processor; Art. 28 DPA must cover instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processors (prior authorisation or objection right), assistance, deletion/return, audits; Art. 30 RoPA must list the AI tool as processing activity/recipient; Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest is the usual basis for internal productivity use, but not where employees input customers' special-category data without a basis; Art. 9 conditions needed for health data; Art. 13/14 transparency; Art. 35 DPIA triggered by EDPB/AEPD high-risk criteria (innovative technology plus e.g. sensitive data, large scale, employee monitoring/evaluation); Art. 22 for automated decisions; Art. 32 security; Art. 33 breach notification within 72h.
- Which parts a vendor product can make easy: pre-signed DPA/SCCs with sub-processor list and change notice; RoPA/DPIA templates pre-filled with tool data flows; policy template and acknowledgement tracking; training-completion evidence for Art. 4; audit logs; admin controls (no-training-on-data, retention settings, DLP/redaction of personal data); EU-region processing. The employer must still own the lawful-basis decision, employee information notice, and the DPIA conclusions.
- Spanish-specific labour-law angle (workers' rights to be informed about algorithmic management, Estatuto de los Trabajadores art. 64.4.d, and digital-disconnection/monitoring rules in LOPDGDD art. 87-91) is background knowledge only and was not researched/cited here.

### Gaps
- No AEPD guide specifically titled for employees' use of third-party AI chatbots was found; AEPD's closest instruments are its staff policy, the agentic-AI guide and the CV-screening warning.
- AEPD policy PDF text could not be extracted; the "no personal/confidential data" rule rests on one press summary.
- DPA/lawful-basis statements above are background knowledge, not cited.

## 3. International transfers: EU-US DPF status, China, Chinese-origin open-weight models hosted in the EU

### Takeaway
The EU-US Data Privacy Framework (Commission decision (EU) 2023/1795) remains valid as of mid-November 2026, but is under live legal and political pressure: the General Court upheld it (Latombe, T-553/23, 3 Sept 2025), an appeal (C-703/25 P) is pending with no hearing or AG opinion announced, and the US Supreme Court's June 2026 Trump v. Slaughter ruling plus PCLOB turmoil have weakened the independence arguments. Transfers to China have no adequacy and have been the basis of enforcement against DeepSeek; self-hosting Chinese open-weight models inside the EU avoids transfer issues but not other duties.

### Cited Findings
- Latombe v Commission: General Court dismissed 3 Sept 2025; appeal lodged 31 Oct 2025 (C-703/25 P) seeking annulment or referral back; one ground attacks the Data Protection Review Court as not a tribunal "previously established by law". As of mid-Nov 2026 no hearing or AG opinion announced; decision plausibly 2027, possibly 2028 (commentators inconsistent). — [Captain Compliance status page](https://captaincompliance.com/education/eu-us-data-privacy-framework-litigation-status-2026/); [InfoCuria C-703/25 P](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/affair?lang=EN&searchTerm=%22T-553%2F23%22&publishedId=C-703%2F25+P); [EDPL article](https://edpl.lexxion.eu/article/EDPL/2026/1/15)
- Trump v. Slaughter (US Supreme Court, 29 June 2026): President may remove FTC commissioners at will; the adequacy decision relied on FTC commissioners being removable only for cause. EDPB Chair Anu Talus asked the Commission on 31 July 2026 to "closely assess" the impact. The Commission is analysing it but has proposed no change; next periodic review set for 2027. — [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/eu-us-data-privacy-framework-litigation-status-2026/); [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/is-the-eu-us-data-privacy-framework-at-risk-the-ftc-ruling-explained-2026)
- PCLOB: three Democratic members removed 27 Jan 2025; reinstatement ordered May 2025; D.C. Circuit heard argument 17 Nov 2026 in LeBlanc v. PCLOB, no decision reported. — [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/eu-us-data-privacy-framework-litigation-status-2026/)
- noyb wrote to the Commission on 30 June 2026 and announced a fresh challenge; as of the latest source not yet filed (single-source, search summary). — [Where things stand 2026 (European Martech)](https://europeanmartech.eu/blog/eu-us-data-privacy-framework-2026-status)
- US vendors can still rely on DPF self-certification; none of the proceedings has suspended the decision. — [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/eu-us-data-privacy-framework-litigation-status-2026/)
- DeepSeek: Italian Garante restricted processing of Italian users' data in Jan 2025; Dutch AP warned users (Feb 2025) and opened an investigation into transfers of personal data to China, coordinating with EU peers; Berlin DPA judged transfers to China unlawful and reported the app to Apple/Google (June 2025; outcome of the app-store request unclear); Belgian consumer group Testaankoop filed a complaint. No AEPD action found. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/03/deepseek-the-countries-and-agencies-that-have-banned-the-ai-companys-tech); [NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/2025/02/01/dutch-data-protection-authority-warns-chinese-chatbot-deepseek); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/12839-dutch-data-protection-authority-investigation-into-cross-border-data-transfers-by-chinese-companies); [MIAI](https://ai-regulation.com/deepseek-one-year-later-regulatory-storm-global-surge/); [Test-Achats](https://www.test-aankoop.be/familie-prive/privacy/pers/testaankoop-dient-klacht-in-bij-de-gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit-tegen-deepseek)
- Self-hosted open weights inside an EU data centre sidestep cross-border-transfer questions; the AI Act does not distinguish by model origin; hosted Chinese APIs (e.g. DeepSeek's own service) are described as non-compliant/to-avoid for personal data. — [Qovery](https://www.qovery.com/blog/eu-ai-act-gpai-enforcement-self-hosting-open-weight-models); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/eu-ai-act-gpai-deepseek-review); [Compare the Cloud](https://www.comparethecloud.net/articles/chinese-open-weight-models-uk-private-deployment) (vendor/consultancy sources)
- Chinese open-weight models are gaining adoption in Europe despite political reluctance. — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3364085/open-weight-chinese-ai-models-gain-foothold-europe-despite-brussels-trepidation)

### Inferences
- Background: absent adequacy, transfers to China need SCCs plus a transfer impact assessment (Art. 46) and, per Schrems II/EDPB recommendations, a realistic assessment of Chinese state access laws — widely viewed as hard to pass; this is why regulators single out DeepSeek's own hosted service.
- Legally, a Chinese-origin model run on EU infrastructure by an EU/US processor is a different risk than the Chinese app: no transfer to China occurs, so GDPR transfer rules are not triggered by origin. Residual issues are commercial/security (model provenance, supply-chain/backdoor concerns, licence, alignment/censorship, buyer perception) and AI Act GPAI provider duties that fall on the model developer, not the SMB deployer.
- Practical buyer posture: DPF "valid but fragile" — SMBs should keep SCCs (and a fall-back plan) in the DPA, not rely solely on DPF.

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation that DeepSeek Italian ban is still in force in Nov 2026 (single blog claim); no AEPD DeepSeek action found; Spanish consumer-agency (OCU/FACUA) request to AEPD reported only via VinciWorks.
- No official EDPB statement on self-hosted Chinese open-weight models was found.
- Details of noyb's announced challenge (forum, filing date) not verified.

## 4. Enforcement actions and fines shaping buyer fear (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Headline enforcement against AI vendors is mixed: the Italian Garante's EUR 15m fine on OpenAI (Dec 2024) was annulled in full by the Rome Tribunal on 18 March 2026 on lead-authority (one-stop-shop) grounds, while DeepSeek faced restrictions and investigations. There is no well-sourced 2025-26 fine specifically for an employer's staff using generative AI; the AEPD acts via warnings/guidance. Buyer fear is driven more by data-leak surveys and regulator warnings than by fines.

### Cited Findings
- Garante fined OpenAI EUR 15m (late 2024); Tribunal of Rome annulled the fine and media-campaign order in full on 18 March 2026, accepting OpenAI's jurisdiction ground (Irish DPC as lead authority after OpenAI Ireland became EEA establishment); only the first of ten grounds was examined; a Garante appeal is possible. Docket numbers differ across sources. — [Cross-Border Data Forum](https://www.crossborderdataforum.org/generative-ai-and-gdpr-enforcement-in-europe-a-lot-of-noise-one-fine-zero-survivors/); [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/openai-prevails-in-landmark-italian-ai-and-gdpr-enforcement-case.html); [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://ntplusdiritto.ilsole24ore.com/art/chatgpt-tribunale-roma-annulla-sanzione-15-milioni-garante-decide-l-autorita-capofila-AI88UIRD); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/italian-court-kills-openais-eur15m-fine-and-it-wasnt-even-close/)
- DeepSeek actions: see section 3 (Italy restriction Jan 2025; Dutch AP investigation; Berlin DPA app-store report).
- Dutch AP (Dec 2025) warned about data breaches via AI chatbots in the workplace. — [Accountant.nl](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2025/12/ap-slaat-alarm-over-datalekken-door-ai/); [Dutch IT Channel](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/news/721439/toezichthouder-waarschuwt-voor-risicos-bij-gebruik-ai-chatbots-op-werkvloer)
- AEPD: warning (not fine) on AI CV screening (Sep 2026); Aena EUR 10m DPIA fine (facial recognition). A commercial blog claims 147 AEPD AI-related files Jan 2025-Mar 2026 — unverified. — [AEPD](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/agencia-recuerda-garantias-para-utilizar-ia-en-analisis-curriculums); [Javadex (low reliability)](https://www.javadex.es/blog/aepd-investiga-uso-ia-empresas-espanolas-privacidad-2026)

### Inferences
- The Rome annulment shows enforcement against an EU-established AI vendor lies with its lead authority (e.g. Irish DPC), reducing the odds of a Spanish authority fining a US vendor directly — but it does not shield the employer-controller, who is directly supervised by the AEPD.
- Buyer fear is therefore about (a) data leakage and professional-secrecy breaches, (b) being the controller who failed to do a DPIA/DPA, and (c) vendor-country/jurisdiction risk — not about a track record of fines on SMBs.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025-26 fine anywhere in the EU on an employer specifically for staff use of generative AI.
- Did not check Garante appeal status after March 2026.

## 5. Spain: AESIA, national AI law, ENS, NIS2

### Takeaway
AESIA is Spain's AI supervisor, but the national AI governance bill (Proyecto de Ley Orgánica para el buen uso y la gobernanza de la IA) approved by Government on 26 May 2026 appears to have lapsed when the Cortes were dissolved for the 29 November 2026 general election (secondary sources; verify). NIS2 is still not transposed in Spain (draft law stalled; RDL 12/2018 still applies). The ENS binds private SMBs only when they supply services to the public sector.

### Cited Findings
- AI bill: Council of Ministers approved the Proyecto de Ley Orgánica on 26 May 2026; published in the Cortes bulletin 12 June 2026; amendments deadline 30 June 2026. AESIA at its core (supervision, single contact point, national sandbox, single complaints window); three-tier penalties up to EUR 35m/7% down to EUR 500k/0.5% (minimums removed per HSF Kramer); size considered for SMEs, preference for corrective measures; public-sector inventory of AI systems; measures for minors and algorithmic transparency; labelling of AI-generated images reported in press. — [Moncloa/Digital Gov press (26 May 2026)](https://digital.gob.es/comunicacion/notas-prensa/mtdfp/2026/05/el-gobierno-aprueba-el-proyecto-de-ley-que-garantizara-una-super.html); [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/05/spain-government-approves-the-draft-organic-law-on-the-proper-use-and-governance-of-artificial-intelligence/); [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/madrid/2026-posts/alerta-espana-avanza-implementacion-reglamento-europeo-ia-junio-2026) (snippet only); [Ara.cat](https://es.ara.cat/economia/gobierno-espanol-da-luz-verde-ley-multara-no-indica-foto-hecha-ia_1_5749191.html)
- Lapse: electoral call for 29 November 2026 ("29N"); bills still in Congress/Senate lapse automatically on dissolution; the AI governance framework bill was in the amendments phase and is listed among those that "die de facto"; data-centre regulation survives because it is a royal decree. — [Euronews Spain (8 Oct 2026)](https://es.euronews.com/2026/10/08/el-otro-29-n-las-leyes-y-promesas-del-gobierno-de-coalicion-que-decaen-con-la-convocatoria); [Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/spanish/las-elecciones-espanolas-del-29-n-dejan-en-el-cajon-las-leyes-de-ia-menores-y-deepfakes/)
- NIS2: deadline 17 Oct 2024 missed; as of 26 Oct 2026 no transposition law; Interior Ministry draft (Jan 2025) never became a bill; caretaker government cannot submit bills; RDL 12/2018 and RD 43/2021 remain; Commission infringement procedure open; possible (not decided) route via royal decree-law. Draft scope: medium and large entities (50+ employees or turnover and balance sheet over EUR 10m) in NIS2 Annexes I/II, plus some regardless of size (telecom, trust services, DNS); draft fines up to EUR 10m/2% (essential) and EUR 7m/1.4% (important). — [Cuatrecasas (26 Oct 2026)](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/propiedad-intelectual/art/ciberataques-sectores-criticos-espera-nis2); [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/draft-law-on-cybersecurity-coordination-and-governance)
- ENS (Real Decreto 311/2022): extends to private-sector entities when they provide services to the public sector by contract; otherwise not mandatory but usable as good practice; certification via ENAC-accredited bodies; conformity audit reportedly every two years for medium/high categories; private providers notify incidents to INCIBE. Sources are mostly certifiers/consultancies. — [Legiscope ENS guide](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/esquema-nacional-seguridad-ens-guia.html); [CNMC document](https://www.cnmc.es/sites/default/files/3625296_0.pdf); [Vodafone ENS page](https://www.vodafone.es/c/empresas/es/nuestra-vision/auditoria-ens/)

### Inferences
- If the Cortes dissolution is confirmed, a new government must re-submit the AI bill, so AESIA's sanctioning powers under a national organic law are unlikely before 2027; AESIA/AEPD/CGPJ competences otherwise rest on the EU AI Act and prior Spanish decrees (background: AESIA was created by RD 729/2023, not researched here). AI Act deadlines for national authorities and penalties are an EU-level compliance gap for Spain.
- Most Spanish SMBs are outside NIS2 (draft thresholds 50+ staff in listed sectors) and outside ENS unless they sell to public bodies; but they are increasingly asked by larger NIS2/ENS-bound customers (supply chain) to evidence equivalent controls. An AI vendor serving public-sector-supplier SMBs may be asked about ENS compliance (the ENS-certified cloud services catalogue, CPSTIC) — background.

### Gaps
- Whether the AI bill formally lapsed and the exact dissolution date (5 Oct 2026 reported by one aggregation) were not verified on BOE/Congreso; one LegalToday result carries inconsistent dates.
- Full text of the AI bill (labelling duties on deployers, worker provisions) not retrieved (HSF Kramer and White & Case returned 403).
- ENS audit periodicity and AI-specific CCN-STIC guidance not verified in BOE/CCN.

## 6. Shadow AI: evidence and recommended responses

### Takeaway
Personal-account use of AI at work is widespread and measured at roughly half or more of generative-AI users in traffic studies, with sizeable minorities pasting work data into public tools. Regulators and enterprises converge on: provide an approved tool, policy plus training, DLP/visibility, and block/limit personal accounts rather than ban outright.

### Cited Findings
- Netskope Cloud and Threat Report 2026 (traffic, Oct 2024-Oct 2025): 47% of employees using genAI do so via personal accounts, down from 78% the prior year; approved-account use rose from 25% to 62%. — [CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/shadow-ai-security-risks-netskope/808880/)
- Verizon 2026 DBIR: 67% of AI users access it via personal unsanctioned accounts on corporate devices. — [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/personal-llm-accounts-drive-shadow)
- PagerDuty/Wakefield 2026 (1,250 office professionals): 43% entered work correspondence into public AI tools. — [PagerDuty](https://www.pagerduty.com/blog/ai/shadow-ai-workplace-survey-2026/)
- Help Net Security (May 2026): 31% of users report no employer training. — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/05/01/shadow-ai-risks-it-oversight/)
- Dutch AP warned on workplace chatbot data-leak risk (see section 4). AEPD staff policy bars personal/confidential data in AI systems (section 2).

### Inferences
- Recommendations drawn from regulator material: sanctioned tool with enterprise terms (Art. 28 DPA, no training on data), written use policy with data classification, AEPD "need-to-know" and minimisation principles, AI literacy training (Art. 4), logging, and DPO/management review. Specific enterprise recommendations (CASB/DLP, browser controls) came from vendor blogs, not cited in detail.
- Statistics differ by method (traffic vs self-report); cite ranges, not one number.

## 7. SMB buyers' security questionnaires and regulated-profession requirements

### Takeaway
Typical AI-tool questionnaires cover certifications (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, increasingly ISO 42001), training-on-customer-data, retention, sub-processors/model provider, data location, deletion and DPA. Regulated professions add professional secrecy (law), Art. 9 health data (clinics) and confidentiality duties; Spanish bar bodies have issued AI guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Common AI vendor questions: certifications and their scope; whether prompts/outputs are used for training (get in writing); retention period and zero-data-retention option; full sub-processor list including underlying model provider, change notice/right to object; data location; deletion and deletion certificates at offboarding; signed DPA; SOC 2 alone does not address AI-specific risks. — [Vanta](https://vanta.com/resources/questions-to-ask-ai-vendors); [Beri](https://www.beri.net/article/ai-vendor-security-review-questions-that-change-the-answer); [Secjuice](https://www.secjuice.com/ai-vendor-vetting-an-ok-practice-guide); [ISMS.online](https://www.isms.online/iso-42001/ai-vendor-risk/) (mostly vendor marketing; treat as indicative)
- Legal: CGAE (Spanish General Council of the Bar) approved an interpretive circular on generative-AI legal filings (April 2026) and a White Paper on AI and the legal profession (Jan 2026; president: 60% of legal professionals already use AI-embedding apps); ICAM (Madrid bar) published a good-practice guide and urged firms to appoint an AI oversight lead; commentary argues secrecy duty goes beyond AEPD data-protection recommendations and that a tech provider processing confidential information is akin to a collaborator of the lawyer. — [El Derecho](https://elderecho.com/la-abogacia-espanola-advierte-de-los-riesgos-de-usar-la-ia-generativa-en-textos-profesionales); [ICAM guide](https://web.icam.es/el-icam-lanza-la-primera-guia-practica-para-un-uso-responsable-de-la-ia-en-la-abogacia/); [Vozpópuli](https://www.vozpopuli.com/legal/el-icam-insta-a-los-bufetes-a-designar-un-responsable-de-supervision-de-ia.html); [Economist & Jurist](https://www.economistjurist.es/noticias-juridicas/pegar-un-contrato-en-chatgpt-puede-poner-en-riesgo-el-secreto-profesional-del-abogado/)

### Inferences
- Background: health clinics/physios process Art. 9 special-category data (needs Art. 9(2)(h) basis, professional secrecy under Spanish patient-autonomy law Ley 41/2002, DPIA almost always required, reinforced security; ENS can apply to public health providers/contractors); accountants/gestorías and real estate agents deal with financial/identity data and AML (Ley 10/2010) confidentiality duties; lawyers owe secrecy under the Estatuto General de la Abogacía. Expect these buyers to ask: no training on data, EU region, DPA that names secrecy duty, sub-processor approval, deletion, audit logs, per-matter/client access controls, ability to disable web search/connectors, PII redaction, and incident notification SLA. These Spanish-law references were not researched/cited here.
- Typical questionnaire items to list for the report: certifications (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001/42001, ENS where public-sector), DPA/SCCs, sub-processors and model provider, hosting region and DPF reliance, no-training guarantee, retention/ZDR, encryption in transit/at rest and key management, SSO/MFA/RBAC, audit logs, pen-testing, breach notification timeline, deletion on exit, AI Act role/ transparency statement, DPIA support, subpoena/government-access policy.

### Gaps
- No Spanish SMB-specific questionnaire corpus; the list is synthesised from international vendor-risk sources.

- CGAE circular text not retrieved.

## 8. EU hosting and "sovereign cloud": legal vs. commercial relevance

### Takeaway
Legally, EU data residency does not by itself satisfy or violate GDPR; what matters is transfers/access by non-EU entities. US-controlled providers remain subject to the CLOUD Act regardless of region, so "EU hosting + non-US provider" is mainly a commercial/risk-reduction signal today, though a Commission sovereignty framework (Cloud and AI Development Act, proposed June 2026) could harden it for public-sector and sensitive procurement.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR Art. 48 limits foreign-court disclosure orders absent international agreements, but the CLOUD Act reaches data controlled by US providers wherever stored; EU-US CLOUD Act talks described as frozen; hyperscaler "sovereign" offerings (Microsoft EU Data Boundary, AWS European Sovereign Cloud under an EU entity) are criticised as not removing US jurisdiction. — [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102o8h0/whose-cloud-is-it-anyway-navigating-data-sovereignty-in-europe); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/01/Data-Localization-and-the-Sovereign-Cloud-EU-Cloud-Regulations-Explained); [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/issue-brief/sovereignty-without-borders-decoding-the-transatlantic-digital-relationship-at-a-time-of-change/); [Andrea Fortuna on AWS ESC](https://andreafortuna.org/2026/01/26/AWS-european-sovereign/)
- Commission proposal for a Cloud and AI Development Act with sovereignty tiers in procurement (published 3 June 2026 per Jones Day; Ropes & Gray describes it as adopted — conflict); would not ban US providers but limit use in sensitive sectors such as health, finance, judiciary. — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commissions-proposed-cloud-sovereignty-framework-creates-new-compliance-tiers-for-software-providers); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102o8h0/whose-cloud-is-it-anyway-navigating-data-sovereignty-in-europe); [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-eu-cloud-and-ai-development-act)

### Inferences
- For private SMBs there is no legal requirement to use a non-US provider or EU-sovereign cloud; GDPR is satisfied by DPF/SCCs plus supplementary measures. The demand is commercial (customer questionnaires, public-sector supply chains, regulated professions' risk appetite, Schrems III fear) and political. Non-US + EU hosting removes both transfer and CLOUD-Act conflicts and de-risks a DPF invalidation, which is a genuine hedge given the pending Latombe appeal and Slaughter ruling.
- Using a Chinese-origin model on EU infrastructure operated by an EU entity avoids both US and Chinese jurisdictional reach on data, but may raise provenance/perception concerns (see section 3).

### Gaps
- Status of the Cloud and AI Development Act (proposal vs adopted) unresolved.
- No source on Anthropic/OpenAI/Mistral EU data residency or zero-retention terms was retrieved; vendor docs must be checked directly.
