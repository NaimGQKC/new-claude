# Sell Proven Controls, Not Borrowed Certifications

**You can credibly sell "enterprise-grade security of the environment and data" to EU SMBs at EUR 8-10 per light user, but only if "enterprise-grade" means a short list of controls you can show a buyer (EU-owned hosting with no-log model providers, SSO through the customer's Google or Microsoft login, no training, a signed DPA with sub-processor list, retention and deletion, encryption and tested backups, an attributable audit trail, a pen test), and not a certificate you do not hold.** Most of that list is configuration of LibreChat, LiteLLM and Caddy that takes days to weeks. The expensive items are SCIM, a LiteLLM-grade admin audit log, and formal attestation (SOC 2 Type I about USD 15-40k, ISO 27001 about USD 25-60k in year one, per vendor-influenced estimates), and none of them is legally required for a private SMB: the law asks for a DPA, an assessed lawful basis, AI literacy measures and, in high-risk cases, a DPIA, not for a sovereign cloud. Against the vendors, the gap you fill is real but narrower than the pitch suggests. ChatGPT Business and Claude Team already give SSO, no-training and a DPA at about USD 20 per seat, yet neither offers SCIM, audit logs or custom retention, and Claude has no first-party EU residency at all; but Google Workspace bundles Gemini with EU data regions at EUR 6.80 per user and Microsoft ships Copilot Chat free with Entra plans, so the pitch is weakest against shops that already pay for those suites. One blocker sits in your own repo: the current MVP routes through OpenRouter, which the project's own docs say does not keep data in the EU, so no EU or sovereignty claim is true until the model calls move to EU-owned, no-log hosts. For interoperability, build in this order: a shared Drive/SharePoint folder (days, no cost), then import of ChatGPT/Claude conversation exports plus a memory-as-Markdown wizard, then a read-only, per-user-OAuth "company brain" MCP server piloted with one customer and one platform, and skip A2A, memory sync and project-object transfer, because no vendor exposes them. Every MCP connection also moves your customer's content into a US vendor's processing, so the "EU-only" claim must be scoped to your workspace, not to the customer's whole AI use.

The second pattern is that the USD 20-25 business tiers converge: ChatGPT Business and Claude Team both need two seats, give SSO with domain verification, and default to no training ([OpenAI Business overview](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8792828-chatgpt-business-overview); [Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). That is exactly the list of things an SMB with a security questionnaire asks about, so a self-hosted product that shows retention settings and a per-user activity export is not behind the cheap tier.

The third pattern, and your sharpest argument, is residency. OpenAI offers European at-rest residency and, since January 2026, in-region inference, but only for new Enterprise and Edu workspaces ([OpenAI data residency](https://help-lb.openai.com/en/articles/9903489-data-residency-for-chatgpt)). Anthropic lists no first-party EU option on any plan; EU processing exists only through AWS Bedrock or Google Vertex ([Anthropic regional compliance](https://claude.com/regional-compliance)), and a third-party review notes Anthropic is not on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework list and relies on 2021 SCCs, although that article contradicts itself on DPF ([Sonomos](https://sonomos.ai/blog/claude-eu-data-residency-2026/)). So an SMB that wants Claude or ChatGPT Business with EU residency cannot buy it at any price near USD 20; that is the gap you can fill.

The fourth pattern is the incumbent bundle, and it is your real competitor for light users. Google folded Gemini into every Workspace Business edition (no separate add-on since January 2025), states that Gemini app prompts are not human-reviewed or used for training, and added EU or US data-region choice per organisational unit on 29 June 2026 ([Google Workspace AI pricing](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/billing/ai-features-and-pricing-updates-for-google-workspace-business-editions); [Workspace Updates, June 2026](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/06/gemini-app-data-regions-support.html)). Microsoft describes Copilot Chat as no extra cost with eligible Entra-based plans and sells Copilot Business at EUR 15.60 (a figure from a search summary of Microsoft's Irish storefront; the page itself returned an error, and a partner source quoted EUR 16.24) ([Microsoft Learn: license options](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-365/copilot/copilot-business-faq); [Primend](https://www.primend.com/blog/important-changes-to-microsoft-licences-and-prices-in-2026/)). Microsoft also has the most documented chain from identity to labels to retention to eDiscovery, with two EU caveats: worldwide traffic can be processed outside the boundary, and Anthropic models used as a subprocessor are excluded ([Microsoft Learn privacy page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy)). For a Google or Microsoft shop, "an AI at EUR 0 extra that already honours our permissions" is hard to beat on price; your case there rests on being non-US, on pay-per-use, and on model choice. Mistral is the credible EU-native alternative but weaker on SMB admin: SAML SSO is Enterprise-only and OIDC is unsupported ([Mistral SSO docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/admin/security-access/sso)), and SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001/27701 are claimed on its help centre ([Mistral help](https://help.mistral.ai/en/articles/323772-do-you-have-soc2-or-iso27001-certification)).

Vendor "enterprise" status has not prevented failures, and you should use that, honestly, as context rather than as a sales weapon. The NYT v. OpenAI preservation order of May 2025 forced retention of consumer, Teams/Business and non-ZDR API logs while carving out Enterprise and Edu ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/06/openai-forced-to-preserve-chatgpt-chats)); a 20-million-log production order was affirmed in January 2026 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/openai-must-turn-over-20-million-chatgpt-logs-judge-affirms)). Prompt-injection and label-bypass flaws hit Copilot (EchoLeak, CVSS 9.3, and a January 2026 bug that let Copilot Chat summarise labelled emails) and Gemini (GeminiJack), all fixed server-side by the vendors ([Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/application-security/researchers-detail-zero-click-copilot-exploit-echoleak); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/18/microsoft_copilot_data_loss_prevention/); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/google-patches-gemini-enterprise-vulnerability-exposing-corporate-data/amp/)). The lesson is that the real control in suite assistants is the customer's own permissions hygiene, which your product does not inherit and therefore does not break: a workspace with no access to the customer's mailbox cannot leak it.

## A credible enterprise bar is eight controls, and most cost days, not dollars

Security questionnaires from SMB buyers converge on the same items: certifications and their scope, whether prompts are used for training (in writing), retention and zero-data-retention options, the full sub-processor list including the model provider, data location, deletion at offboarding, and a signed DPA ([Vanta](https://vanta.com/resources/questions-to-ask-ai-vendors); [Secjuice](https://www.secjuice.com/ai-vendor-vetting-an-ok-practice-guide); mostly vendor marketing, so treat as indicative, as no Spanish SMB questionnaire corpus was found). Regulated buyers add professional secrecy: the Spanish bar (CGAE) issued a circular and white paper on generative AI, and ICAM urged firms to appoint an AI oversight lead ([El Derecho](https://elderecho.com/la-abogacia-espanola-advierte-de-los-riesgos-de-usar-la-ia-generativa-en-textos-profesionales); [ICAM](https://web.icam.es/el-icam-lanza-la-primera-guia-practica-para-un-uso-responsable-de-la-ia-en-la-abogacia/)). Synthesising these with the control research, a minimum credible set is: (1) SSO through the customer's identity provider with IdP-enforced MFA and no local sign-up; (2) EU residency end to end, including the model host; (3) a DPA with SCCs fallback and a sub-processor list with change notice; (4) written no-training and no-log guarantees traceable to each model host; (5) retention and deletion the customer can set, plus a deletion-at-exit procedure; (6) encryption in transit and at rest and restore-tested encrypted backups; (7) an attributable audit trail of who used what; and (8) an independent pen test with a vulnerability-disclosure process. SOC 2, ISO 27001 and ENS are deliberately not on this list; section four explains why.

### What the stack gives you cheaply, with effort, or only with money

The self-hosted stack delivers most of this natively. LibreChat supports OIDC, SAML, LDAP, email and passkeys; `ALLOW_EMAIL_LOGIN=false` removes the local login form; allowed email domains restrict who can register; and for Entra, group gating works through `OPENID_REQUIRED_ROLE` ([LibreChat auth docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/configuration/authentication); [Entra guide](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/configuration/authentication/OAuth2-OIDC/azure)). Two traps matter for non-technical customers. OpenID and SAML "don't support disabling registration alone", so anyone who authenticates at the IdP can be auto-created unless you gate by domain or required role; and the Google guide documents no Workspace hosted-domain restriction, so domain control rests on the allowed-domains list ([LibreChat Google guide](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/configuration/authentication/OAuth2-OIDC/google)). SAML has no single logout and no documented group mapping, so prefer OIDC. MFA is delegated: LibreChat's 2FA applies only to local users, so enforce MFA in Google or Entra and keep local 2FA for the break-glass admin ([LibreChat auth docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/configuration/authentication)).

Two licensing facts shape the build-versus-buy decisions. First, LiteLLM publishes no enterprise price; its enterprise page positions the tier toward teams with 100+ users, gives free admin-UI SSO up to five users, and gates audit logs, SCIM, JWT auth, secret managers, key rotation and per-key guardrails behind a `LITELLM_LICENSE` ([LiteLLM enterprise](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/enterprise); [LiteLLM pricing](https://www.litellm.ai/pricing)). A competitor puts it at about USD 30,000 per year, which is unverified and biased ([TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/litellm-enterprise-pricing-tco-vs-truefoundry)). The five-user allowance probably covers the two founders, who are the only LiteLLM admin users, so you need the license only if a customer demands LiteLLM-level audit logs or SCIM. Second, the LibreChat admin panel is a separate service, requires LibreChat v0.8.5 or later, offers custom roles and a grant audit log, and its repository is AGPL-3.0 ([Admin panel docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/configuration/admin_panel); [repo](https://github.com/ClickHouse/librechat-admin-panel)). AGPL's network-use clause means that if you modify the panel and expose it to users, you must offer them the modified source; unmodified internal use imposes little, but this is general knowledge to confirm with a lawyer, and the main LibreChat license was not re-verified in this research (the project's earlier decisions doc recorded it as MIT, with the panel as the exception). Your earlier decision to skip the panel and configure through YAML stays sound because the panel is young (114 commits, no releases at the time) and you gain a licensing question for little value.

The supply chain deserves more attention than certificates. On 24 March 2026 LiteLLM PyPI versions 1.82.7 and 1.82.8 were backdoored after a compromised Trivy GitHub Action leaked a publishing token, exfiltrating SSH keys, cloud credentials and environment variables; safe versions are 1.82.6 and earlier, and the exposure window is reported as several hours by Heise and shorter by other sources ([Heise](https://heise.de/-11224139); [Snyk](https://snyk.io/articles/poisoned-security-scanner-backdooring-litellm); [JFrog](https://research.jfrog.com/post/litellm-compromised-teampcp/)). LiteLLM answered with isolated CI, PyPI Trusted Publishing and Cosign-signed images from `v1.83.0-nightly`, and recommends pinned versions ([LiteLLM CI/CD v2](https://docs.litellm.ai/blog/ci-cd-v2-improvements)). Whether the official Docker image shipped the bad versions was not verified. LibreChat had its own 2026 CVEs: CVE-2026-31951 let user-created MCP servers steal OpenID access tokens (fixed in 0.8.3-rc2), and CVE-2026-32625 (CVSS 9.6) allowed secret exfiltration through MCP server URL injection, with affected range not retrieved ([NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/cve-2026-31951); [Rapid7](https://www.rapid7.com/db/vulnerabilities/cve-2026-32625/)). The practical rules are to pin digests, block user-added MCP servers, set `allowedDomains` for any MCP target, and publish a patch cadence; all of them are cheap and are things a buyer's security reviewer will check.

### The model host is your real security boundary

The weakest link in an "EU-only" promise is not your VM but the model API. The eu_hosting notes show wide variance: IONOS states prompts are never logged, are processed only in Germany, with no third-party sub-processors and BSI C5 scope ([IONOS data handling](https://docs.ionos.com/cloud/ai/ai-model-hub/governance-and-compliance/data-handling)); Regolo claims zero retention by design ([Regolo](https://regolo.ai/zero-data-retention/)); Nebius retains prompts by default for speculative decoding unless ZDR is toggled and its public endpoints are "Global", not EU-pinned ([Nebius legal](https://docs.tokenfactory.nebius.com/legal/legal-quick-guide)); and Mistral's EU endpoint covers chat completions but excludes agents, batch and files from its regional guarantees ([Mistral regional inference](https://docs.mistral.ai/inference/regional-inference)). Ownership matters as much as location, since a US-controlled provider is reachable by the CLOUD Act wherever it stores data ([Cornell LII, 18 U.S.C. 2713](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2713)); the Commission's April 2026 sovereign-cloud award placed OVHcloud (in a consortium), STACKIT and Scaleway at SEAL-3 ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/commission-advances-cloud-sovereignty-through-strategic-procurement-2026-04-17_en)). The earlier hosting research recommends T-Systems, Mistral EU, OVH, STACKIT, Scaleway and Berget with ZDR providers first, and no OpenRouter, US hyperscaler regions or Nebius serverless. Two of your current design choices conflict with the pitch. The MVP's OpenRouter route, and the "smart" lane that sends public web reading to DeepSeek's API, both put content outside the EU; the second is subtler because search queries and fetched-page requests are derived from user prompts and can carry client context. For customer workspaces, turn the DeepSeek lane off by default and make "Boost" (closed US models) an explicit, labelled, admin-controlled opt-in.

### Single-tenant per customer is the right default for two people

LibreChat now has `TENANT_ISOLATION_STRICT` and a tenant header option, but several settings (for example enforced two-factor) are process-wide, the first account becomes ADMIN in single-tenant mode, and the Mongo and Postgres layers stay shared; no independent pen test or audit of its multi-tenancy was found ([LibreChat upgrade docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/configuration/upgrade); [LibreChat auth docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/configuration/authentication)). A pooled deployment also concentrates every customer's provider keys in one blast radius, and the LiteLLM compromise stole environment variables from hosts ([JFrog](https://research.jfrog.com/post/litellm-compromised-teampcp/)). A per-customer stack (separate compose project, databases, LiteLLM keys and encryption keys on a small EU VM) gives the cleanest isolation and deletion story: terminate the stack, destroy the volume, issue a deletion statement. The project's cloud-deploy notes put a 4 GB Hetzner server at about EUR 6-10 per month. My arithmetic on the earlier cost model (a light user costs about USD 1.72 per month in EU-only mode) shows the unit economics: ten seats at EUR 9 bring EUR 90, against about EUR 10 for the VM and about EUR 16 of model cost, leaving roughly EUR 64 before backups, support and VAT; three seats bring EUR 27 against about EUR 15, leaving roughly EUR 12. So single-tenancy works if you set a minimum monthly platform fee (a five-seat floor is a reasonable starting point), and the operational cost is update fan-out, which scripted templates and Renovate-style pinning absorb for tens of customers, not hundreds. Offer a pooled trial tier with clear disclosure if you must, but do not sell it as "enterprise-grade".

## Law asks for a DPA, a DPIA and AI literacy, not for a sovereign cloud

The employer SMB is the GDPR controller and the vendor is a processor under Article 28 for business tiers; consumer tiers make the vendor an independent controller, which is the legal core of the shadow-AI problem. In your case you are likely the customer's processor and your model hosts are your sub-processors, so the DPA you sign and the sub-processor chain you disclose are the contractual heart of the product (this role analysis is background GDPR knowledge, not re-verified in the research, and should be confirmed by counsel). EDPB Opinion 28/2024 says model anonymity is case-by-case, legitimate interest needs the three-step test, and a deployer must assess the lawfulness of the model it deploys, with a conversational assistant cited as a plausible legitimate-interest use ([EDPB Opinion 28/2024](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf)). The AEPD has no guide on staff use of third-party chatbots, but its adjacent instruments give you templates: an internal generative-AI policy for its own staff (reported rule: no personal or confidential data into any AI system, from a press summary because the PDF could not be extracted) ([AEPD press release](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/agencia-publica-politica-interna-uso-inteligencia-artificial)); a February 2026 agentic-AI guide requiring need-to-know access, per-processing memory compartmentalisation, strict retention, integrity-protected logs and DPO involvement ([Cuatrecasas analysis](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/latam/propiedad-intelectual/art/guia-aepd-inteligencia-artificial-agentica)); and a September 2026 warning on AI CV-screening demanding a prior DPIA, transparency and genuine human intervention ([AEPD warning](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/agencia-recuerda-garantias-para-utilizar-ia-en-analisis-curriculums)). The agentic guide's compartmentalised memory and integrity-protected logs map almost one-to-one onto your audit-trail and per-user memory design, which is a useful point to make to a security-minded buyer.

The AI Act is a timing story. The Digital Omnibus was adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 and has been in force since 27 July 2026: standalone Annex III high-risk duties move to 2 December 2027 and product-embedded ones to 2 August 2028; Article 4 AI literacy stays but softens to a duty to "take measures to support" staff literacy; Article 50 transparency applies from 2 August 2026, with legacy generative systems getting until 2 December 2026 for machine-readable marking ([Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-delay-high-risk-december-2027/); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act)). A general drafting assistant is low risk, but repurposing it for candidate scoring would land in Annex III, which is why the AEPD CV warning matters. Two consequences follow for you. Evidence of literacy measures (training modules, acknowledgements) is the practical artifact customers need, and a product that ships it removes a real chore. And because your workspace generates text, images and files under your brand and was placed on the market after 2 August 2026, you may count as a provider of a generative AI system with marking and disclosure duties under Article 50, with no grace period (my reading of the secondary sources; the official text was not retrieved, so get a lawyer's view before launch).

International transfers are the reason non-US, EU-hosted architecture is a hedge, not a legal necessity. The EU-US Data Privacy Framework remains valid: the General Court upheld it on 3 September 2025 and the appeal (C-703/25 P) has no announced hearing, but the US Supreme Court's June 2026 Trump v. Slaughter ruling on removing FTC commissioners and PCLOB turmoil have weakened the independence arguments, the EDPB chair asked the Commission to assess the impact, and noyb announced a new challenge ([Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/eu-us-data-privacy-framework-litigation-status-2026/); [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/is-the-eu-us-data-privacy-framework-at-risk-the-ftc-ruling-explained-2026)). EU residency does not itself satisfy or violate GDPR; what matters is non-EU access, and US-controlled providers stay reachable through the CLOUD Act even in EU regions ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102o8h0/whose-cloud-is-it-anyway-navigating-data-sovereignty-in-europe)). For a private SMB, DPF plus SCCs plus supplementary measures is lawful today; a non-US stack removes both the transfer question and the DPF-invalidation risk, so it is a commercial and risk hedge. Chinese-origin open-weight models hosted by an EU operator on EU hardware sidestep China-transfer rules, which is what regulators attacked in DeepSeek's own hosted app (Italian restriction, Dutch investigation, Berlin DPA report); the residual issues are provenance, licence terms and buyer perception, and no EDPB statement on self-hosted Chinese open weights was found ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/03/deepseek-the-countries-and-agencies-that-have-banned-the-ai-companys-tech); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/eu-ai-act-gpai-deepseek-review)).

Spain adds little binding for a typical SMB. The national AI governance bill (AESIA at its core) was approved by Government on 26 May 2026 but appears to have lapsed with the Cortes' dissolution for the 29 November 2026 election, so national sanctioning powers are unlikely before 2027 ([Euronews Spain](https://es.euronews.com/2026/10/08/el-otro-29-n-las-leyes-y-promesas-del-gobierno-de-coalicion-que-decaen-con-la-convocatoria); lapse not verified against the BOE). NIS2 is still untransposed (draft scope: 50+ staff or EUR 10m in listed sectors) ([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/propiedad-intelectual/art/ciberataques-sectores-criticos-espera-nis2)). The ENS (Real Decreto 311/2022) binds private firms only when they supply the public sector, with Categoría Básica a self-declaration and Media/Alta needing an ENAC-accredited certificate ([Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/esquema-nacional-seguridad-ens-guia.html)); no EU-owned AI inference host with confirmed ENS status was found. Enforcement shaping buyer fear is mostly about leaks, not fines: the Italian EUR 15m OpenAI fine was annulled by the Rome Tribunal on 18 March 2026 on lead-authority grounds ([WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/openai-prevails-in-landmark-italian-ai-and-gdpr-enforcement-case.html)), the AEPD fined Aena EUR 10m for a missing DPIA on facial recognition (a secondary summary) ([Ecija](https://www.ecija.com/actualidad-insights/mas-sanciones-y-de-mayor-importe-la-aepd-sube-el-nivel-de-multas-en-2025/)), and no verified fine exists anywhere in the EU for an employer's staff use of generative AI.

### Shadow AI is the demand driver, and no vendor tier fixes it alone

Measured shadow use is high and falling slowly: Netskope's 2026 traffic study found 47% of generative-AI users on personal accounts, down from 78%, with approved-account use up from 25% to 62% ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/shadow-ai-security-risks-netskope/808880/)); Verizon's 2026 DBIR reports 67% of AI users on unsanctioned personal accounts on corporate devices ([Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/personal-llm-accounts-drive-shadow)); and a 1,250-person survey found 43% had entered work correspondence into public tools ([PagerDuty](https://www.pagerduty.com/blog/ai/shadow-ai-workplace-survey-2026/)). Methods differ (traffic versus self-report), so quote ranges. The Dutch AP warned about workplace chatbot leaks in December 2025 ([Dutch IT Channel](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/news/721439/toezichthouder-waarschuwt-voor-risicos-bij-gebruik-ai-chatbots-op-werkvloer)). The convergent response is an approved tool, a written policy with data classification, literacy training and visibility, not a ban. Be honest about what SSO can do: it blocks unmanaged sign-ups on the corporate domain and gives admins offboarding, but domain capture does not catch personal gmail.com logins ([LastPass](https://blog.lastpass.com/posts/shadow-ai-is-an-identity-problem)). Catching those needs network or browser discovery such as Entra Global Secure Access ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-sg/Entra/global-secure-access/concept-shadow-ai-discovery)), which you do not provide. So the claim you can make is "we give staff an approved, cheap place to work so they have less reason to go elsewhere", not "we eliminate shadow AI". The low price helps with a related point: the cheapest route to shadow AI is a EUR 8 Go subscription, and your product wins only if it is nearly as easy to start.

## Claim what you can show: attestations cost USD 15-60k and are not yet needed

Certification costs are wide and vendor-influenced. SOC 2 Type I is the cheapest attestation (USD 7,500-60,000 per Drata, USD 12-40k per SOC2Auditors; a lean first Type I about USD 15-40k all-in), and Type II needs a 3-12 month observation window ([Drata](https://drata.com/learn/soc-2/type-1-vs-type-2); [SOC2Auditors](https://soc2auditors.org/insights/soc-2-type-1-vs-type-2/)). ISO 27001 certification audits run about USD 8-20k for 10-50 employee firms, with year-one totals of USD 25-90k or more and surveillance audits at 60-70% of the Stage 2 cost ([heyData](https://heydata.eu/en/magazine/iso-27001-costs-2026-certification-sme); [Consilien](https://consilien.com/news/iso-27001-certification-cost-timeline)). Compliance-automation tools add roughly USD 7,500-30,000 per year (Secureframe from about USD 7,500; Vanta estimates USD 10-30k for startups; all unofficial), with EU-based options such as Kertos, Secfix and DataGuard ([SOC2Auditors Vanta](https://soc2auditors.org/insights/vanta-pricing/); [Kertos](https://www.kertos.io/en/compliance-hub/eu-alternatives-vanta-drata)). ENS Media costs about EUR 8-25k per one consultancy, third-party only ([Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/esquema-nacional-seguridad-ens-guia.html)). A small, single-product, single-tenant scope on an already-certified host shortens any audit, but inheriting a host's certificate is not the same as holding one. SOC 2 is mainly a US-buyer demand; for Spanish and EU SMBs ISO 27001 plus GDPR evidence matters more, and ENS matters only if customers supply public bodies.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| "Hosted in the EU" | True only after model calls leave OpenRouter and any US endpoint; today the MVP does not meet it |
| "Your data is never used to train models" | Say it only per provider, with the written terms on file for each model host and your own policy |
| "SSO with your Google or Microsoft account, MFA enforced by your IdP, no separate passwords" | After configuration (days); verify domain gating works for OIDC |
| "You set retention; deletion on request and at exit" | After configuration plus a documented deletion procedure; memories and backups need stated handling |
| "Per-customer isolated environment" | After the single-tenant template exists |
| "Encrypted in transit and at rest; restore-tested backups" | After disk encryption and one documented restore test |
| "Independent penetration test" | After the test, and say the date and scope |
| "Admin and user activity log" | After building the audit pipeline; do not call it a full audit log until it records who asked what with integrity protection |
| "Aligned with GDPR, with DPA and sub-processor list" | After counsel reviews the DPA; "GDPR-compliant" as an absolute is a legal conclusion you should avoid |
| Must NOT claim | "SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / ENS certified or compliant"; "enterprise-grade" without a feature list; "sovereign" in the Commission's SEAL sense (a host's rating is not yours); "zero data retention" unless every provider in the chain has it written; "prevents shadow AI" or "prompt-injection-proof"; "SCIM" or "SIEM export" before they exist; "no US exposure" while any US-controlled provider, Boost model or DPF-reliant tool is in the path |

A realistic sequence for two people is a trust page, `security.txt`, DPA, sub-processor list, written policies and the controls above in weeks 0-6; a third-party pen test and a fix cycle in months 2-4; and a certification decision after that, driven by named deals, not by hope. Sources for the cheaper trust signals (trust-page tools, cyber insurance, DPA templates) were not found, so those costs are open.

## Interoperability: shared folder first, MCP brain second, everything else later

### The constraint that governs every design

Power users will keep using Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, and the fixed constraint is who can add a custom connector on which plan. Claude supports custom remote MCP connectors on all plans, but on Team only Owners add them, on Enterprise Owners and custom roles do, and on Pro and Max the individual adds it; connections originate from Anthropic's cloud, so the server must be public on port 443, with `https://claude.ai/api/mcp/auth_callback` allow-listed ([Anthropic: custom connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp)). In ChatGPT Business only admins and owners can enable developer mode and publish custom MCP apps, and an admin cannot enable it for individual members; Enterprise adds RBAC delegation ([OpenAI help, via search summary](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-developer-mode-and-mcp-apps-in-chatgpt)). ChatGPT needs Streamable HTTP, supports no API keys, client credentials or customer mTLS, and offers only per-user OAuth with CIMD preferred and DCR as fallback ([OpenAI Apps SDK auth](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/build/auth)). Gemini Enterprise needs a creator with `roles/discoveryengine.editor` and an org admin to override a default-on block, caps enabled tools at 100, and needs a pre-registered OAuth client ([Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/connectors/custom-mcp-server/set-up-custom-mcp-server)); no source showed custom remote MCP in the consumer Gemini app. Microsoft wraps an MCP server as a declarative-agent plugin with OAuth or Entra SSO, needs a tenant admin to enable custom-app upload, and needs a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence for agents ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/extensibility/build-mcp-plugins)). Mistral Le Chat requires an admin to configure MCP connectors ([Mistral docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/le-chat/knowledge-integrations/connectors/mcp-connectors)).

The consequence for an SMB is that the plans most companies buy for governance (Claude Team, ChatGPT Business) are the ones where a power user cannot self-serve, while the plans where they can (Claude Pro/Max, reportedly ChatGPT Plus/Pro) are personal accounts, which is the shadow-AI position you are trying to reduce. Expect one admin approval per platform per customer, and no per-seat fee for custom connectors was found beyond the plan itself.

### Option comparison

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Shared Drive/SharePoint folder | Days | Existing suite licences | Oversharing becomes AI-searchable; stale uploaded copies | High: they edit Docs/Word as now | Build first |
| (b) Company-brain remote MCP server | 3-6 weeks for a read-only pilot | Small hosting; one admin approval per platform | Tool poisoning, prompt injection via shared notes, public endpoint, data leaving to US vendors | Low for setup (admin once), moderate after | Pilot after (a), one platform |
| (c) Import/export of conversations, memory, instructions | 1-2 weeks wizard | Low | Imported content untrusted; lossy memory | Medium with a wizard | Build second |
| (d) SSO/identity federation | Days to weeks | Zero | Does not catch personal accounts | High (invisible) | Do in phase 0 |
| (e) Standards (A2A, Apps SDK, Agent Plugins) | Watch only | Zero | Immature | None | Skip for now |

### (a) A shared knowledge folder is the common ground that already works

Drive and SharePoint have official connectors in every major assistant that run with the user's own permissions. ChatGPT's connectors respect existing Drive and SharePoint permissions, with synced connectors for Drive (Team/Enterprise/Edu) and SharePoint (Pro/Business/Enterprise/Edu) ([OpenAI SharePoint connector](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12143177-sharepoint-connectors-in-chatgpt); [Varonis](https://www.varonis.com/blog/chatgpt-enterprise-connectors)). Claude's Microsoft 365 connector is Team and Enterprise only, needs an Owner plus an Entra Global Admin, and is delegated ([Claude M365 connector](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-using-the-microsoft-365-connector)); its Drive connector signs in per user ([Claude Drive connector](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/google/drive)). Gemini Gems use Drive files as knowledge, and Copilot agents ground on SharePoint and OneDrive content the user can access ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/extensibility/knowledge-sources)). The recipe is one folder (for example "Team AI Context") holding `context.md`, `instructions.md`, `decisions.md` and source documents, shared by group so one ACL governs everything, with each tool's project instruction reading "start with folder X". Non-technical staff keep editing in Docs or Word and nothing is re-uploaded.

It has known breakpoints. Claude's Drive connector is disabled in shared projects (via a search summary), ChatGPT project files are capped at 40 in Business/Enterprise/Edu with reported memory and file-library quirks in shared projects, Gems cannot share NotebookLM sources, each tool indexes separately so freshness varies, and permission inheritance means colleagues can get different answers ([Claude help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9519177-add-content-to-projects); [OpenAI Academy](https://academy.openai.com/en/public/clubs/work-users-ynjqu/resources/projects); [Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/09/gem-sharing-gemini-app-workspace.html)). The gap that matters for you is the reverse direction: whether LibreChat can read the same folder. LibreChat is documented as an MCP client with OAuth and per-user connections ([LibreChat MCP docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/features/mcp)), but whether official Google Drive, SharePoint or Notion MCP servers are available and authenticate acceptably was not verified, so the fallback is a periodic sync of the Markdown folder into LibreChat's file search, with stale copies as the cost. Also, the same oversharing that makes Copilot dangerous applies: a connector surfaces whatever the user can reach, so a folder-permissions check belongs in onboarding.

### (b) A company-brain MCP server is feasible but moves data out of your boundary

The current spec is MCP 2026-07-28, which makes Streamable HTTP stateless (no sessions or initialize handshake), deprecates HTTP+SSE, Roots, Sampling, Logging and Dynamic Client Registration in favour of Client ID Metadata Documents, and keeps OAuth 2.1 with mandatory Protected Resource Metadata and audience-bound tokens ([MCP changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog); [MCP authorization](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization)). Anthropic says support is "being rolled out" with no dates ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/bringing-mcp-2026-07-28-to-claude)), and per-client support status was not found, so serve both the stateless and the 2025-11-25 behaviour. One server that is public HTTPS on 443 at a stable `/mcp`, Streamable HTTP only, with RFC 9728 metadata, S256 PKCE, `resource` and audience validation, CIMD plus a DCR fallback and a pre-registered-client path, and allow-listed redirect URIs for claude.ai, chatgpt.com, Google's `vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com` and Teams, covers all four platforms (this synthesis is mine). A practical wrinkle is that CIMD needs the authorization server, not just the MCP server, to fetch and validate client metadata, so expect to front Google or Entra login with a small authorization server of your own; whether Google or Entra alone can serve that role was not established.

LibreChat appears only as a client in its documentation, so the brain is a separate service that LibreChat also connects to, with a small adapter if projects and memory must be exposed (no existing LibreChat-as-MCP-server feature was found) ([LibreChat MCP docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/features/mcp)). Open-source memory servers exist (mcp-memory-service; Memlord, which is AGPL-3.0 with a commercial option), but their maturity, licences and activity were not verified ([mcp-memory-service](https://pypi.org/project/mcp-memory-service/); [Memlord listing](https://mcpservers.org/servers/myrikld/memlord)). A hybrid is cheapest: your thin server holds notes, decisions and project instructions that do not live in Drive, and the vendors' own Drive/SharePoint connectors handle documents (my judgement, not sourced).

The security design must treat shared notes as an injection channel. OWASP advises treating each MCP server as an untrusted domain because models see all tool descriptions, enabling cross-server poisoning, and an arXiv study of seven clients found strong guardrails in some (Claude Desktop) and high cross-tool poisoning susceptibility in others (Cursor) ([OWASP MCP cheat sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/MCP_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html); [arXiv 2603.21642](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21642)). The AEPD's "rule of two" (no more than two of uncontrolled input, unrestricted sensitive access and automatic real-world actions) is a good guardrail, though the attribution is unconfirmed. So: per-user OAuth with identity-based ACL filtering (a shared service account collapses everyone to the broadest privilege and ChatGPT cannot use one anyway), read-only tools by default, a separate confirmed write tool, short static versioned tool descriptions, a low tool count, provenance labels in outputs, and a log of every call with user, client, tool and resource. Most importantly, once a Claude or ChatGPT user pulls a note through your server it is processed by a US vendor under that vendor's plan terms, which on Pro or Max may allow training unless the user opts out ([Anthropic consumer terms](https://anthropic.com/news/updates-to-our-consumer-terms)). Give admins a data-class switch (what may leave) and say plainly in the product that your EU guarantee ends at the connector.

### (c) Import and export are lossy, so move text, not objects

Chat history is the only reliably portable item. LibreChat imports ChatGPT, Claude, ChatbotUI and LibreChat JSON by auto-detection (default 250 MiB file limit), and exports one conversation at a time as Markdown, text, JSON, CSV or PNG ([LibreChat import docs](https://www.librechat.ai/docs/features/import_convos)); Open WebUI auto-detects ChatGPT exports ([Open WebUI docs](https://docs.openwebui.com/features/chat-conversations/data-controls/import-export.md)). The ChatGPT export is a ZIP of partitioned `conversations-NNN.json` files whose `mapping` tree must be walked to linearise branches, a layout known only from community reverse engineering, and ChatGPT Business, Enterprise and Healthcare users cannot self-serve the export at all ([chatgpt-to-markdown](https://difegam.github.io/chatgpt-to-markdown/explanation/export-format); [OpenAI export help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7260999-exporting-your-chatgpt-history-and-data)). Claude's export excludes uploaded files, does not mention projects or memory, and cannot be imported into another personal Claude account; Team export needs an owner to enable it ([Claude export help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9450526-how-to-export-your-claude-data)). Memory moves only as a text summary produced by a prompt: Claude imports and exports memory that way on all plans, ChatGPT has no standalone memory export, and Gemini's memory and chat import is personal-account only and unavailable in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK ([Claude memory import](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12123587-importing-and-exporting-your-memory-from-chatgpt-and-other-ai-providers); [Google Gemini import](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16868299?hl=en)). Projects, Custom GPTs, Gems and Copilot agents have no portable format.

What travels is plain files: Markdown instructions, source documents, SKILL.md folders and MCP server URLs. Agent Skills became an open standard in December 2025, and on 22 October 2026 Anthropic donated it to the Agentic AI Foundation, claiming more than 70 platforms including ChatGPT, Cursor and GitHub Copilot (self-reported) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-agent-skills-to-the-agentic-ai-foundation)); install paths and host-specific fields still differ ([cross-agent guide](https://serhiismetanskyi.github.io/digital-garden/ai-skills/06-cross-agent-compatibility/)). AGENTS.md is a repo convention for coding agents and is not read by chat-app Projects; Claude Code's fallback to it rests on secondary reports ([InfoWorld](https://infoworld.com/article/4224410/claude-code-now-also-accepts-instructions-in-openais-agents-md-format.html)). So the build is a wizard that exports user-owned Markdown (instructions, a reviewed memory summary), points to the shared folder, installs skills, and optionally imports ChatGPT or Claude history into LibreChat; it is a mover, not a sync. Legal portability levers are weaker than people expect: GDPR Article 20 covers an individual's data provided by them and does not oblige vendors to produce importable projects, and the EU Data Act's cloud-switching chapter (applying since 12 September 2025) is the stronger lever for a company leaving a SaaS vendor, though its reach over AI chat products is untested ([EDPB WP242](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-on-the-right-to-data-portability-under-regulation-2016679-wp242_es); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/law-in-tech/2026/cloud-exit-under-the-eu-data-act)).

### (d) and (e) Identity helps at the margin; standards are mostly not for you yet

Pick one IdP (Google or Entra) as the source and enforce it everywhere: ChatGPT's "Enforce SSO" blocks password and social login for verified-domain users but is documented for Enterprise/Edu, not Business, and a Business admin cannot force personal-account migration ([OpenAI SSO FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11489188-sso-for-chatgpt-business-faq)); Claude adds domain capture, "Require SSO" and restrictions on creating organisations on the verified domain (from a secondary how-to) ([T-Minus 365](https://tminus365.com/how-to-set-up-claude-sso-with-microsoft-entra-id-10-minute-guide/)). That identity layer is also what makes the shared-folder and MCP designs safe, because connector permissions are only as good as the IdP groups behind them. On standards: MCP, Agent Skills and AGENTS.md now sit under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation; A2A reached v1.0 on 9 April 2026 with 150+ supporting organisations and reportedly moved into the foundation in August, but no named production adopters or Claude/ChatGPT support was found, and Copilot Studio's A2A status is "public preview" or "rolling out" depending on the source ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year); [Axios](https://axios.com/2026/08/17/a2a-agentic-ai-foundation-open-ai-standards)). The OpenAI Apps SDK is built on MCP, and Agent Plugins (published 6 August 2026, a `plugin.json` bundling MCP servers and skills) is described by critics as thin and omits Anthropic from its listed backers ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-agent-plugins-open-standard-skills-mcp)). For non-technical users none of these is visible; they matter only because one MCP server and one skills folder can serve many tools.

### Recommended phasing

Phase 0 (weeks 0-6, security foundations that unblock every claim): move all customer-data model calls to EU-owned, no-log hosts; OIDC with domain gating and IdP-enforced MFA; retention settings; disk encryption and one restore test; the DPA, sub-processor list and trust page; pinned images and a patch cadence; a single-tenant deployment template; and the shadow-AI kit (policy template, AI-literacy module, acknowledgement tracking). Phase 1 (months 1-3): the shared-folder playbook (folder template, group permissions, a pre-connector oversharing check), the import wizard for ChatGPT and Claude history, memory-as-Markdown export, the first version of the audit pipeline, and a third-party pen test. Phase 2 (months 3-6): a read-only company-brain MCP pilot with one willing customer and one platform, probably Claude because individuals on Pro/Max can self-add and Team Owners can approve, with ChatGPT Business second; a data-class switch and call logging from day one; decide on attestation based on actual deals. Phase 3 (later, demand-led): SCIM, ISO 27001, ENS if public-sector suppliers appear, a SKILL.md library, and Gemini and Copilot connectors. Skip for now: A2A, cross-vendor memory sync (no vendor reads an open memory format), project-object transfer (no API), multi-tenant pooled hosting as a security selling point, the LibreChat admin panel, and building your own audit-grade SIEM export.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the question. "Enterprise-grade security" is not a certificate you buy or a tier you copy; at the SMB end it is eight controls and a paper trail, and the vendors' own cheap tiers lack half of them, which is your opening. But the same research shows that the two strongest objections are not about security at all: the incumbents' suite bundles make the light-user price comparison unfavourable for Google and Microsoft shops, and any interoperability you offer sends customer content to a US vendor's processing, so the sovereignty story and the interop story pull against each other and need an admin-visible data-class switch.

The most useful reframing is that your credibility will come from sequence and specificity: fix the hosting claim first (the MVP does not yet meet it), publish the controls list, get the pen test, and let named deals pull attestation, rather than buying ISO 27001 hoping it creates demand. On interoperability, the cheap, reliable layer is a shared folder and moving text between tools, because every vendor supports files and none supports projects, memory or agents as portable objects; the MCP brain is a second-stage pilot to run with one friendly customer, where you can measure whether power users actually use it before committing to the public endpoint, the OAuth work and the per-platform admin approvals it requires.
